## ⚠️ Alerts & outages

**The City of Cape Town has scheduled water shutdowns and pressure tests in several suburbs from Monday 23 to Sunday 29 November for pipe replacements, valve checks and repairs, Cape Town ETC reports.** Helderrand is off from 09:00 to 16:00 on Monday. On Tuesday 24 November parts of Woodstock, including Victoria, Pine, Salmon, Warwick, Queens, Woodlands and Ravenscraig roads, are off from 09:00 to 18:00, and Hanover Park has low pressure from 09:00 to 14:00. On Wednesday 25 November Canal Road in Wetton is off from 08:00 to 20:00, streets around Dreyer Street in Claremont from 09:00 to 18:00, and parts of Schaapkraal from 08:00 to 18:00; an emergency repair on a bulk pipeline in Papkuilsvlei Road, Philippi, from 09:00 to midnight may cut Schaapkraal's supply and lower pressure in Strandfontein. On Thursday 26 November parts of Brackenfell, including Vredekloof, Arauna, Morgenster, St Kilda, Eden Park and Okavango Park, are off from 10:00 to 18:00. Zero-pressure tests, which can mean low pressure or no water, are planned in Diep River (Monday), Protea Park and Wesfleur in Atlantis (Tuesday), Montclair, Milnerton, Brooklyn, Century City and Summer Greens (Wednesday), and Tamboerskloof and Gardens (Wednesday and Thursday). The City advises storing water in clean, sealed containers and keeping taps closed during shutdowns; updates and tanker locations are posted on @CityofCTAlerts on X.

## 🚨 Safety

**A 20-year-old man died and a police officer was wounded in a shooting in Lingelethu West at about 03:10 on Thursday 19 November, police told Cape Town ETC.** SAPS said a murder case has been opened and that the Independent Police Investigative Directorate (IPID), which investigates deaths involving police, has taken over the investigation. Police did not give details of what led to the shooting and referred further questions to IPID.

## 🏛️ Local government

**The City has opened public participation on its Integrated Development Plan (IDP) for 2027 to 2032, the five-year plan that sets the new council's priorities, and residents can comment until 20 January 2027, Cape Town ETC reports.** The City is asking for residents' concerns and priorities, both in their own neighbourhoods and across the city, through an online survey that can be completed once per person. The IDP feeds into each year's budget, so what is raised can influence which projects are funded. A process plan for drafting the IDP was on the agenda of the new council's first sitting on Thursday 19 November.

## 🚌 Getting around

**Metrorail monthly tickets will cost R35 to R46 more from Tuesday 1 December, after PRASA confirmed a phased fare increase on 19 November, Cape Town ETC reports.** A monthly ticket for up to 15 km rises from R180 to R215, 16 to 40 km from R220 to R266, 41 to 60 km from R250 to R291, and 61 km or more from R280 to R322. Single and weekly tickets stay the same for now. A second, inflation-linked increase on all tickets is planned from 1 April 2027 at the earliest, followed by yearly increases of about 4% to 4.5%. PRASA had first proposed increases of R60 to R80 and reduced them after objections; according to GroundUp, the commuter group United Commuters Voice said the final figures match what was agreed at a meeting on 16 October. PRASA says the extra money will go towards energy, maintenance and security.

## 🩺 Health & community

**The Western Cape Department of Health and Wellness says recent cases of meningococcal disease, a bacterial infection that can cause meningitis, do not on their own mean there is an outbreak, Cape Town ETC reports.** The department says the disease occurs in South Africa all year, and that it investigates each case, traces close contacts and gives them preventive antibiotics where needed. Warning signs include sudden high fever, severe headache, a stiff neck, sensitivity to light, vomiting, confusion and a red or purple rash that does not fade when pressed; in babies, poor feeding, unusual floppiness or drowsiness, cold hands and feet and a bulging soft spot. Babies under three months may be seriously ill without a high temperature. Anyone with suspected symptoms should get medical help immediately.

## 🌊 Environment

**Cape Town's dams held 69.3% of capacity on Friday 20 November, down from 70.4% a week earlier and well below the 83.8% recorded at the same point last year, according to City figures reported by The Citizen.** Every dam dropped in the week to 16 November. Voëlvlei is at about 49%, compared with nearly 94% a year ago, and Steenbras Lower at about 45%. Only Berg River and Steenbras Upper are higher than last year. Level 1 water restrictions are due to begin on 1 December.
