## 🚨 Safety

**Four men threw a petrol bomb at the Eerste River home of an Anti-Gang Unit officer shortly after midnight on Monday 19 October, and one suspect died after a police chase.** According to the Independent Police Investigative Directorate (Ipid), the officer returned home with colleagues after his wife called him. The suspects fled by car and allegedly fired at police, who returned fire. When the car stopped, one man ran a few metres, collapsed and was declared dead at the scene; the cause is not yet clear and Ipid has taken over that investigation. The other suspects were arrested, and Kleinvlei detectives are investigating arson. About 13 hours later, nearby, five people threw stones at a police vehicle, cutting two officers on their hands and faces. No arrests have been made in that case, Daily Maverick reports.
{: #eerste-river-attack }

> [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-19-masemola-returns-as-thugs-petrol-bomb-anti-gang-cops-home-attack-other-police/)

**A six-year-old boy died after being hit by a Golden Arrow bus in 35th Avenue, Elsies River, on Friday 16 October.** He died later at Tygerberg Hospital. Police are investigating a case of culpable homicide. Golden Arrow said the bus was on schedule and the child allegedly ran into its path, and that the driver went to a police station for her safety after alleged threats from community members. Police have not yet given their account of how the collision happened.
{: data-place="35th Avenue, Elsies River" }

> [Cape Town ETC](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/six-year-old-boy-dies-after-golden-arrow-bus-collision-in-elsies-river/)

**A 17-year-old has been arrested on two counts of murder over a shooting in Lower Klipfontein Road, Athlone, on 24 August.** The Anti-Gang Unit arrested him at the Vygieskraal informal settlement on Sunday 18 October, and he was due in the Athlone Magistrate's Court on Monday. Separately, officers on patrol at Vygieskraal arrested a man found with an unlicensed 9mm pistol and 15 rounds of ammunition.

## 🏛️ Local government

**The City is setting up a specialist office to handle large, complex development applications, which it expects to open by February 2027.** The Strategic Development Applications Hub will be based at the Table Bay district planning office in the CBD and deal with high-value projects that fall outside routine land-use and building-plan applications. The City's eight district planning offices will keep handling routine applications and get more staff in the coming months. Deputy Mayor Eddie Andrews said the aim is shorter turnaround times. The City also plans to phase in automated and AI-assisted processing for minor, low-value applications.

## 🗳️ Elections 2026

**The ANC will not name its mayoral candidates, including for Cape Town, before the 4 November election.** The party's head of elections, Mdumiseni Ntuli, told Daily Maverick it had decided not to reveal mayoral candidates anywhere in the country, citing limited time and the risk that disappointed contenders could affect campaigning. The party had earlier promised the names in August, then September. Daily Maverick reports that eight names had been shortlisted for Cape Town and Mangaung; none has been made public.

## 🏠 Housing & development

**The City has finished R83 million of roads, water, sewer and electrical services for 86 restitution homes at Protea Village in Bishopscourt, and families are expected to move in during the second half of 2027.** Protea Village residents were forcibly removed under the Group Areas Act between 1959 and 1970. The City has released about R320 million of municipal land for the project, including 50 serviced sites that will be sold on the open market to help pay for the restitution homes. Four hectares of greenbelt along the Liesbeek River will stay public. The next steps are the outstanding building approvals and construction. Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis marked the milestone with the families' communal property association.

## 🩺 Health & community

**The Western Cape Government has asked national government for a coordinated response to shortages of medicines at public clinics.** Health MEC Mireille Wenger first raised stock-outs in June and wrote to national Health Minister Aaron Motsoaledi in September asking for action with clear timelines. The province blames manufacturing and regulatory bottlenecks, supplier limits, import and customs delays, and slow testing and release of some medicines. It says it is monitoring stock at the Cape Medical Depot weekly, moving stock between facilities and arranging temporary imports. Patients may be given an alternative or a smaller quantity; the department asks them to keep collecting their medicine, ask if it looks different, and not change how they take it without speaking to a health worker.
{: #medicine-shortages }

> [Western Cape Government](https://www.westerncape.gov.za/health-wellness/article/western-cape-calls-national-action-medicine-supply-challenges-continue), [Cape Town ETC](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/wcs-urgent-appeal-for-national-response-to-ongoing-medicine-supply-woes/)

**Parents at Kuyasa Primary in Khayelitsha want a permanent fix for sewage problems after an overflow in August suspended classes for days.** The City repaired the sewer outside the school and the Western Cape Education Department cleaned and sanitised the grounds, which a health inspection has since declared safe. The governing body wants a dedicated sewer line, separate from the surrounding community's, ideally installed in the end-of-year holidays, and expects to meet the provincial infrastructure department about it. The education department says the latest blockage was inside the school's own system, and the City says bricks, rubbish and paper are often to blame. The school has more than 1,500 learners.
{: data-place="Kuyasa, Khayelitsha" }

> [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/some-relief-for-kuyasa-primary-but-sanitation-worries-remain/)
