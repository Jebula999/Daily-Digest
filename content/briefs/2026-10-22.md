## 🚨 Safety

**More than 20 immigrant informal traders have gone to the Western Cape High Court against the City of Cape Town, SAPS and anti-immigrant groups, saying they have been blocked from trading in Bellville since 30 June.** Represented by the Legal Resources Centre and supported by the Somali Association of South Africa, they say attacks at the Bellville trading zone and Middestad Mall have destroyed or stolen their stock, sometimes with law enforcement present, and that only foreign nationals were targeted. They want the court to find that the City failed in its duty under the Informal Trading By-law and that the police response was unconstitutional, to order a joint protection plan within ten days, and to interdict leaders of March and March, Operation Dudula and a concerned-citizens group. The City said it does not condone intimidation or violence and that criminal complaints should go to SAPS; police said two people had been arrested for public violence at the Bellville bus terminal and that extra deployments are in place. No hearing date has been reported.

**SAPS in the Western Cape received 175 new vehicles on Wednesday at the launch of its festive season operations.** Police plan intelligence-led operations in crime hotspots with Cape Town Metro Police, LEAP officers and other agencies, aimed at arrests of wanted suspects and seizing illegal firearms. Provincial Police Oversight and Community Safety Minister Anroux Marais called the vehicles "a much-needed boost to policing capacity" and asked residents to report crime information. The provincial statement does not say how the vehicles will be shared among stations.

## 🩺 Health & community

**The Western Cape Education Department has warned job seekers about fake adverts for teaching posts and for paid matric exam assistants.** Social media posts claim young unemployed people can earn R8,230 marking Grade 12 scripts in December, which the department says is false; it named sateachers.co.za and careerstime.co.za as sites carrying false adverts. Warning signs include being asked to apply through a private Gmail address or to pay a fee: the department says it never asks for payment for applications, interviews or appointments. Genuine educator vacancies are on the WCED's e-recruitment system, and exam marking posts on its National Senior Certificate marking page; school governing body posts are advertised by the schools themselves.

## 🌊 Environment

**A R106 million rooftop solar installation at a distribution centre in Brackengate will supply three Cape Town properties through the City's electricity grid.** Redefine Properties' 7MW system, with 11,691 panels on about 57,000 square metres of roof, is expected to produce about 9,889 MWh a year for Kenilworth Centre, Blue Route Mall and the Towers office precinct. It works through "wheeling": power is fed into the municipal grid at the Rubicon substation and credited to the other sites. It is part of the City's wheeling pilot, and is described as the first rooftop installation built for wheeling on the City's grid. Energy Mayco member Xanthea Limberg welcomed it.
