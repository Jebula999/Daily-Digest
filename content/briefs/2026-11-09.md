## ⚠️ Alerts & outages

**The City has scheduled water supply interruptions in several suburbs from today, Monday 9 November, to Thursday 12 November for pipeline connections, valve work and network testing, Cape Town ETC reports.** Plumstead loses water from 09:00 to 19:30 on three days: today in Woodgate, Southfield, Severn, Basil and Medway Roads; on Tuesday in Dalziel, Meadow, Montley and Denbigh Roads; and on Thursday in the area bounded by Morton, Tiverton, Exeter, Thornbury and Hemyock Roads. Steenberg has shut-offs from 08:00 to 20:00 on Tuesday and Thursday. On Tuesday, parts of Loevenstein, Lotus River, Bantry Bay, Elsies River and Hanover Park are also affected, and on Wednesday Buitengracht Street in the CBD is shut off from 09:00 to 18:30 while a meter connection is installed between Military Road and Leeuwen Street. Rondebosch East, Rylands, Lansdowne and nearby areas can expect low pressure on Thursday ahead of a new water main due to be connected by Saturday 14 November. The City advises storing water beforehand, keeping taps closed during the work, and following @CityofCTAlerts for updates and tanker locations.

## 🗳️ Elections 2026

**The IEC declared the official results of the 4 November local elections on Sunday 8 November, confirming that the DA kept its outright majority in Cape Town.** According to The Citizen's table of final metro results, the DA took 56.42% in the city and the ANC 13.95%, followed by the Patriotic Alliance (3.77%), the EFF (3.73%), the Freedom Front Plus (3.42%), ActionSA (2.75%), Al Jama-ah (2.38%), GOOD (2.29%) and the Cape Coloured Congress (2.23%). Final seat numbers for the new 235-seat council are on the IEC's results site. Nationally, turnout was 52%, up from 45.9% in 2021, and 127 of the 213 municipalities have hung councils, according to SAnews. Under the Municipal Structures Act, each new council must hold its first meeting within 14 days of the results being declared; that is where the mayor and speaker are elected.

## 🚨 Safety

**A 40-year-old man was due in the Bellville Magistrates' Court today, Monday 9 November, after police found 5,000 mandrax tablets in a bag at the Mabel Road bus depot in Bellville, Cape Town ETC reports.** Police said the tablets, found during a routine patrol at about 08:20, have an estimated street value of R250,000.
{: #bellville-mandrax data-place="Bellville" }

> [Cape Town ETC](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/police-seize-5-000-mandrax-tablets-at-bellville-bus-depot/)

**Five fruit producers from the Robertson and Montagu districts are due in the Western Cape High Court today, Monday 9 November, in an urgent bid to make Premier keep taking their fruit after the closure of its Tulbagh cannery, GroundUp reported.** The farmers grow peaches and apricots under supply agreements first signed with RFG Foods, which Premier took over. They want an interim order requiring Premier to receive, grade and pay for their fruit in the 2026/27 season while the contract dispute goes to arbitration, arguing that the agreements need at least two harvest seasons' notice to end. Separately, the Competition Commission wants the Premier–RFG merger approval revoked; Premier denies withholding its closure plans.
{: #tulbagh data-place="High Court, Keerom Street, Cape Town" }

> [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/fruit-farmers-take-premier-to-court/), [Daily Maverick: Competition Commission](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-07-premier-group-rhodes-merger/)

## 🚌 Getting around

**Provincial Mobility Minister Isaac Sileku is visiting nine taxi ranks from 04:30 this morning, Monday 9 November, as part of an oversight programme, Cape Town ETC reports.** He starts at Site C and Makhaza in Khayelitsha, then goes on to ranks in Mfuleni, Delft, Bellville, Nyanga, Langa, Mutual and Maitland to look at how they operate. A follow-up meeting between government and the taxi associations is set for Tuesday 10 November.

## 🩺 Health & community

**The Cape of Good Hope SPCA received 81 calls on Guy Fawkes night, up from 51 last year, with eight inspectors working through the night and at least five animals needing urgent veterinary care, Daily Maverick reports.** One of them, a dog in Delft, was fatally injured on a spiked security gate while trying to escape the noise of fireworks on 5 November. The City's Law Enforcement Department logged 344 fireworks-related incidents over three days, more than double last year's 156.
