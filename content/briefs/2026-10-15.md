Three weeks before the 4 November local government election, housing is the story Cape Town keeps returning to: who gets public land, how fast homes get built, and where people sleep tonight.

## Minister asks City to pause sale of Annandale farm near Dunoon

National Human Settlements Minister Thembi Simelane has asked the City of Cape Town to pause its planned sale of the Annandale farm near Dunoon, [News24 reports](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/news/human-settlements-minister-orders-cape-town-to-halt-prime-land-sale-pending-housing-probe-20261014-0809). The headline says the halt is pending a housing probe. The full article is behind a paywall, so the scope of that probe and the City's response aren't yet clear.

Annandale is a site of more than 70 hectares in Milnerton, across the N7 from Dunoon. In August the City put it out to developers for a mixed-income, public-private development. Human settlements Mayco member Carl Pophaim said it could deliver at least 5,000 homes, with rentals of roughly R3,500 to R10,000 a month, [according to EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/08/14/up-to-10k-homes-planned-for-new-neighbourhood-close-to-dunoon). Developers have until **21 October** to submit proposals.

The site has become an election issue. At Wednesday night's mayoral debate, GOOD's Brett Herron called for public land to stop being sold off. The DA's Eddie Andrews defended the City's housing record, citing more than R10 billion invested in a single financial year, [the Cape Argus reported](https://iol.co.za/capeargus/news/2026-10-15-cape-town-housing-crisis-dominates-mayoral-debate/). Eleven parties took part.

## Kensington night shelter expands to 200 beds

Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis opened the expanded Haven night shelter in Kensington on Thursday. It now has 200 beds, up from 80, after a R12 million upgrade, [EWN reports](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/10/15/cape-town-mayor-geordin-hill-lewis-opens-expanded-200-bed-night-shelter-in-kensington).

The City says it has added about 640 Haven beds over five years, bringing the total to more than 1,300. It has set aside R100 million for homelessness programmes this financial year, with more shelter space planned in Kuils River, Wynberg and District Six.

## One dead in Observatory informal settlement fire

A man died when fire destroyed at least three structures in the Singabalapha informal settlement in Observatory early on Wednesday. City Fire and Rescue said the blaze was put out at 03:55. Several residents were displaced, and the cause is not yet known, [GroundUp reported](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/man-dies-in-fire-in-observatory-informal-settlement/). Formal identification of the man is awaiting DNA results.

## In brief

- **Nappy shortages at clinics.** Disability Revolution and #UniteBehind say adults who rely on free incontinence products from clinics are often given far fewer than they need, and are asking the Western Cape health department and the City for a clear written policy. The City says no client with an assessed need is refused, and that patients can ask to be reassessed. [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/disabled-people-say-clinics-give-them-too-few-nappies/)
- **Robben Island free for children until 31 October.** South African children aged 15 and under travel free with a paying adult on the 09:00, 11:00 and 13:00 tours, under the museum's NextGen Heritage pass. Check the details when booking on the Robben Island Museum website. [Getaway](https://www.getaway.co.za/news/children-can-visit-robben-island-free-until-october-31/)
- **Planning tribunal fines and approvals.** The City's north-eastern planning tribunal approved flats in Belgravia, Bellville, and fined property owners up to R150,000 for unauthorised building work and land use. [Read what they decided](/stories/2026-10-15-mpt-north-eastern-decisions/).
