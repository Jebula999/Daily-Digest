## 🗳️ Elections 2026

**Ballots from Wednesday's local government elections are being counted, and the provincial IEC expects provisional Western Cape results on Friday 6 November, SABC News reports.** By 02:00 on Thursday about 3% of the province's voting districts had been declared, and around 15 of its 30 municipalities were showing progress, with results coming in slowly. Nationally, more than 5% of votes had been counted by early Thursday. About 1.5 million people had voted in the Western Cape by 17:00 on Wednesday, according to IEC figures reported by Daily Maverick, and 148,493 special votes cast on 2 and 3 November are being counted with them. There are 2,158,976 registered voters in Cape Town. Results are published as they are declared on the IEC's results site.

## 🚌 Getting around

**The intersection of Duinefontein Road (M10) and Govan Mbeki Road (M9) near Phola Park is partly closed from 05:00 today, Thursday 5 November, until midnight on Friday 13 November, for MyCiTi Phase 2A construction, the City said.** Southbound traffic on Duinefontein Road cannot go straight through towards Jakes Gerwel Drive and can only turn right towards Lansdowne or left towards Gugulethu, and northbound traffic from Jakes Gerwel Drive cannot enter the intersection. Drivers on Jakes Gerwel Drive heading for the eastbound lanes of Govan Mbeki Road should use Heinz Road. Urban mobility mayoral committee member Rob Quintas asked people to avoid the intersection where possible and allow extra time.

**Seven battery-electric buses have arrived for MyCiTi, bringing the City's electric fleet to eight, with 13 more due in December and 17 between January and March 2027, TygerBurger reports.** The 59-seat Volvo buses have bodies designed and built in South Africa. If plans hold, 21 of them will run on the N2 Express between Mitchells Plain, Khayelitsha and the city centre. Rob Quintas said the buses should lower operating costs at a time of high diesel prices, and that buying more depends on national funding.
{: #e-buses }

> [TygerBurger](https://novanews.co.za/tygerburger/cape-towns-firts-electric-buses-arrived-more-expected/)

**Someone dies on Cape Town's roads on average every 11 hours and 40 minutes, according to the City's latest Traffic Crash Report.** Between July and September, traffic services recorded more than one million offences, executed 29,710 warrants and impounded 1,574 public transport vehicles, People's Post reports. Law Enforcement and Metro Police issued 118,416 traffic fines, 138% more than before, and arrested 686 drunk drivers. Safety and security mayoral committee member JP Smith blamed a lack of accountability on the roads and warned that year-end traffic will add pressure.

## 🚨 Safety

**Three men were arrested on Monday 2 November for allegedly stealing copper cable from a national key point in Bothasig, TygerBurger reports.** Police cable-theft officers and a neighbourhood watch responded to a report from a radio control room. The cable is valued at about R120,000, with a further R60,000 in damage to the property. The men face charges of damaging essential infrastructure and possessing suspected stolen cable, and were due in the Bellville Magistrates' Court on Tuesday 3 November.

## 🌊 Environment

**The City has started a R15 million, six-month pilot that pumps salty groundwater from under the beach into the upper Milnerton Lagoon to dilute pollution, and a UCT scientist and residents say it does not tackle the cause, GroundUp reports.** Professor Kevin Winter said the pilot may raise oxygen and salinity, but that "dilution of pollution is not the solution". The Milnerton Central Residents Association pointed to repeated sewage spills from the Potsdam Wastewater Treatment Works this year. City data show water quality has improved since 2024, but E. coli at Woodbridge reached 141,000 per 100ml on 8 September, far above the 4,000 safety limit for canoeing. The City says the pilot does not replace the R5.2 billion Potsdam upgrade, which it says is 80% complete and due to finish next year. A separate dredging plan faces eight appeals, with a decision expected later this year.
{: #milnerton-lagoon data-place="Milnerton Lagoon" }

> [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/concerns-over-r15-million-plan-for-milnerton-lagoon/)

**Western Cape citrus growers and catamaran builders are weighing the effect of a proposed suspension of South Africa's AGOA trade benefits with the United States from 1 January 2027, People's Post reports.** The Cape Chamber of Commerce and Industry said exporters are diversifying but that this takes time, and that the date leaves room for more talks between Pretoria and Washington. The Citrus Growers Association raised concerns about supply chains and margins, and Exporters Western Cape asked government to work with affected industries.
