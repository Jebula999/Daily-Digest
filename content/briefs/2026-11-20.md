## 🏛️ Local government

**Geordin Hill-Lewis was re-elected executive mayor at the first meeting of the new City council on Thursday 19 November, with Xanthea Limberg elected executive deputy mayor with 131 votes and Felicity Purchase re-elected speaker, Daily Maverick reports.** The DA holds 133 of the council's 235 seats, the only outright majority in any of the country's eight metros. In his address, Hill-Lewis repeated five pledges: stronger policing, including a metro police detective unit, more jobs, affordable and reliable services, more housing and a cleaner city, Cape Town ETC reports. He said 14 sites are earmarked for affordable housing and 39 more are being prepared. "Cape Town must, and will, show the rest of South Africa what is possible," he said, according to Daily Maverick. ANC councillor Lwazi Phakade, whose party is the main opposition with 33 seats, congratulated him and said the ANC would be "constructive, but robust", naming housing, informal settlement upgrades and jobs as urgent. GOOD's Roscoe Palm criticised reports that Hill-Lewis had considered a move to Johannesburg; Hill-Lewis told radio station 702 he had made no such proposal. The FF Plus's Pieter Jansen van Vuuren raised rising rates and the coming Level 1 water restrictions.

## 🚨 Safety

**Deputy President Paul Mashatile did not commit to national support for a City of Cape Town detective academy when DA MP Glynnis Breytenbach asked about it in Parliament on Thursday 19 November, Daily Maverick reports.** The City wants investigative powers for its metro police, which cannot currently investigate crime, and in August the mayor declared an intergovernmental dispute with acting Police Minister Firoz Cachalia over the issue. Mashatile said the City and national government needed to collaborate and suggested it work through NatJoints, the national security co-ordinating structure. He said Operation Prosper, the R823 million police and army operation against gangs and illegal mining that began in March, would continue and be strengthened, and that more than 4,000 people had been arrested under it.

## 🏠 Housing & development

**The City's south-western planning tribunal on Tuesday 17 November approved a temporary relocation area in Gugulethu and a phased housing subdivision in Grassy Park, and refused a higher-density rezoning in Claremont and a subdivision in Rosebank, according to the City's published resolutions.** The tribunal, which rules on rezonings and departures from building rules, also approved a hotel at Boulder's Place in Simon's Town and a business rezoning on Belvedere Road, Claremont, and set more than 30 penalties for unauthorised building, the largest R50,000 for a property on Military Road, Steenberg, where a cellphone mast was put up without approval. ## 🩺 Health & community

**National shortages of about 50 critical public-sector medicines are disrupting treatment for patients in Cape Town, with some chronic patients reportedly going up to three months without supplies, Daily Maverick reports.** Medicines affected include epilepsy, psychiatric and Parkinson's drugs, some TB medicines, oral contraceptives, cancer drugs and some vaccines. At the Community Care Clinic in Vrygrond, which serves patients from Vrygrond, Retreat, Lavender Hill and Hanover Park, a project manager said the clinic is 25% fuller than normal. The Ward 72 councillor said residents had repeatedly left Retreat Community Health Centre without their medication. The Western Cape Department of Health and Wellness said in October that its two-to-three-month stock buffer had been used up; it is monitoring stock weekly at the Cape Medical Depot, moving stock between facilities and sourcing alternatives, and has called for national intervention. Health MEC Mireille Wenger has written to the national health and transport ministers about the shortages and port delays.

## 🌊 Environment

**Small recycling buyback hubs run from homes and garages on the Cape Flats have diverted more than 3,000 tonnes of waste from landfill since 2023 and created more than 500 jobs, according to the nonprofit RLabs, GroundUp reports.** RLabs' ReCha programme trains young people to run the hubs, most of them in Mitchells Plain, Strandfontein and Khayelitsha, and gives them scales and an app to record what they collect. Waste pickers are paid between R1 and R17 a kilogram depending on the material, and RLabs collects the waste and takes it to a larger buyback centre. About 125 people have started their own hubs.
