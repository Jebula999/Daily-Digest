## 🚨 Safety

**Police seized 69,000 Mandrax tablets, with an estimated street value of R5.5 million, at a storage facility in Grassy Park early on Thursday.** According to SAPS, the National Intervention Unit and Crime Intelligence searched the premises on 8th Avenue at about 01:40 after a tip-off, finding 69 bags of 1,000 tablets each. A 58-year-old woman was arrested for possession of drugs and is expected to appear in the Wynberg Magistrates' Court today, Friday 16 October.

## 🏛️ Local government

**A council appeals committee has overturned a subcouncil's refusal to let a Parow tavern keep five limited-payout gambling machines.** Subcouncil 6 had refused the business licence in August on socio-economic grounds. At its 9 October meeting the General Appeals Committee voted 8–0 to uphold the appeal for Le Lieu Sports Tavern at 197A Voortrekker Road, noting that the venue has held a provincial gambling licence since 2005 and that the application met the Businesses Act. Members said the subcouncil had not given enough reasons for a refusal.
{: data-place="Parow" }

## 🚌 Getting around

**National Treasury is phasing out the grant that has paid for MyCiTi's growth, and the City says that leaves the bus service's future after 2028 uncertain.** The Public Transport Network Grant ends by 2027/28; Treasury is moving the money to passenger rail after the finance minister said the grant hadn't improved access enough for what was spent. Cape Town still has R2.2 billion of a R7.1 billion allocation, available until June 2028, earmarked for the Metro South-East route to the Cape Flats due to open in July 2027. Mayoral committee member Rob Quintas told GroundUp that MyCiTi has grown 68% since 2021, and that the City wants long-term, performance-based funding. #UniteBehind says the City's draft transport plan now provides for 70 buses on the new route instead of 221, and warns that fares or services could suffer.
{: #myciti-grant }

> [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/doubts-over-the-future-of-myciti-buses-as-treasury-ends-grant/)

## 🩺 Health & community

**The City has set targets to reduce the number of people sleeping rough by 25% by 2030, measured against this year.** It also aims to cut new arrivals on the streets by 25%, shorten the average time people spend sleeping rough by 20%, and make sure at least 30% of people helped off the streets haven't returned two years later. A pilot citywide count of homeless people is planned for November, with a full count in 2027, and the City is building a shared database with partner organisations to track who gets help.
{: #homelessness-targets }

> [Cape Town ETC (City of Cape Town release)](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/cape-town-homelessness-strategy-sets-2030-targets/)

**From next year, every Grade 1 class in the Western Cape will be taught maths through Bala Wande, a programme that teaches numeracy in children's home languages.** About 112,000 learners will be reached in 2027, after a pilot this year with about 8,000, the Western Cape Education Department said. Lessons are offered in isiXhosa, English and Afrikaans, and 3,467 Grade 1 and 2 teachers were trained in September and October. Grade 2 follows in 2028 and Grade 3 in 2029.
{: #bala-wande }

> [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/western-cape-schools-to-teach-arithmetic-in-isixhosa-from-next-year/)

**Renaye Kramer, who produced *District Six: The Musical* and *Kat and the Kings*, has died in Cape Town at 74.** She ran the business side of David Kramer's productions for about four decades and co-founded the Dock Road Theatre at the V&A Waterfront in 1990, which took no state funding. *Kat and the Kings* went on to London and Broadway and won the 1999 Olivier Award for Best New Musical.
