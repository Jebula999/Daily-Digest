## 🗳️ Elections 2026

**Special voting at 217 voting stations in Cape Town could not start on time on Monday 2 November because special-vote ballots had not been delivered, the IEC said.** The stations, in 33 wards from Kraaifontein and Atlantis to the City Bowl, Khayelitsha, Wynberg, Constantia, Delft, Athlone, Gugulethu, Philippi and Strand, opened as ballots arrived, and the IEC later said all of them were operating. Western Cape electoral officer Michael Hendrickse apologised and said the problem was confined to the metro and was caused by delivery delays despite the efforts of the contracted printer. Affected stations stayed open until 18:00. Today, Tuesday 3 November, is the last day of special voting, from 08:00 to 17:00, and the IEC says approved special voters who have not yet voted can do so today. Nationally, about 780,000 special votes were cast on Monday.

**Polling day is tomorrow, Wednesday 4 November, a public holiday, with voting stations open from 07:00 to 21:00.** Campaigning must stop at midnight tonight. Voters approved for a home visit should stay at the address on their application today, as officials will not come back a second time. To check your voting station, SMS your ID number to 32810 or send "Hi" to the IEC on WhatsApp at 060 088 0000.

## 🚨 Safety

**The taxi associations CATA and CODETA have agreed to a week of calm and to an independently facilitated process to deal with the causes of their route conflict.** The agreement came at a meeting on Monday 2 November with SANTACO Western Cape, the City and the provincial government. Separate meetings between each association and officials, guided by a facilitator, start next week, and a follow-up meeting with government is set for Tuesday 10 November. The Palm taxi rank in Delft, closed after a shooting on 28 October in which two people were injured, had not been reported as reopened.
{: #taxi-calm }

**The Western Cape High Court has convicted a former police constable of murder and of offences under the organised crime law for passing police information to a leader of the 28s Mobsters gang.** The court found he also scouted a target and sold a firearm later linked to the gang. ## 🏠 Housing & development

**Families in the Siyakhana informal settlement next to Oscar Mpetha Road in Khayelitsha still have no taps or toilets, three years after the City said it was looking for ways to help, GroundUp reports.** Residents collect water from a fire hydrant, some walking about 15 minutes each way, and use buckets for sanitation. The families settled in 2020 on land earmarked for the Mahama infill housing project in Mandela Park; a City survey this year counted about 43 structures. Mayoral committee member for human settlements Carl Pophaim said in October that the City is investigating options and identifying land for relocation, and that taps and toilets need the landowner's permission. The national Department of Public Works and Infrastructure said talks with the City are ongoing and that it plans to transfer the property to the City and the Housing Development Agency.
