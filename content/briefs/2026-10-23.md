## 🏛️ Local government

**Pensioners and social grant recipients who have not yet applied or renewed their property rates rebate have until 30 November 2026, the City said on Thursday.** All rebates have to be renewed after the 2025 general valuation, and approved rebates run from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2029. The City said it is handling high volumes of applications and apologised to those still waiting; until an application is approved, the account is charged in full, and the rebate is then credited back to 1 July 2026. Forms are available at walk-in centres and on the City's website, and can be handed in at a walk-in centre or emailed to rates.rebate@capetown.gov.za. Renewals can also be done through the City's WhatsApp line on 060 042 5572. Life right holders over 60 in separately valued sectional title schemes, with a household income of up to R27,000, also qualify.

**The south-western planning tribunal on Tuesday refused to allow subdivisions at Glendirk Farm on Klaassens Road in Constantia and at Breton Close in Hout Bay.** It approved subdivisions or rezonings for subdivision elsewhere in Constantia and in Newlands and Southfield, rezoned a Dean Street property in Rondebosch for flats, and set penalties of R20,000 and R15,000 for unauthorised work in Constantia and Hout Bay. All the published decisions were unanimous; outcomes for a large Philippi farm application and 14 other penalties have not yet been published. **The City's Special Events Committee has approved up to R8.35 million in support for minstrel, Malay choir and Christmas band events this summer.** The largest amount, up to R4.2 million, goes to the 2de Nuwe Jaar Minstrel Parade on 2 January, including R2 million for DHL Stadium hire and other costs. The Kaapse Klopse Karnival Association's competitions get up to R2 million and the Cape Malay Choirs road march up to R1.3 million, with smaller sums and venue fee waivers for other choir and band boards. The money depends on organisations meeting financial and corporate compliance criteria. Safety and Security Mayco member JP Smith said the events are "an important part of Cape Town's identity and heritage", and the City's events permit office asks organisers to apply promptly.

## 🏠 Housing & development

**The City handed over 67 subsidised homes at the Kanonkop Phase 2 project in Atlantis on Wednesday and Thursday.** The project will deliver 422 Breaking New Ground (BNG) houses in all, allocated through the City's housing needs register, and Human Settlements Mayco member Carl Pophaim said handovers will continue over the coming months. No cost was given.

## 🩺 Health & community

**The first phase of a R32 million upgrade at Mandela Park Stadium in Khayelitsha is complete.** The athletics track has been resurfaced, the jumping and throwing areas upgraded, and the roof, columns and access ramps repaired; new seating is being installed. The second phase covers lighting, paving and toilets, and the City says it aims to finish before the coming athletics season, without giving a date. The goal is World Athletics Class Two certification, which the Blue Downs and Wesfleur tracks already have.
{: #mandela-park-stadium data-place="Mandela Park Stadium, Khayelitsha" }

**Two Cape Town restaurants, Fyn and Salsify at the Roundhouse, have each climbed 17 places on the extended list of The World's 50 Best Restaurants 2026.** Fyn is now 65th and Salsify 71st on the 51–100 list published on Wednesday. The top 50 will be announced in Lima, Peru, on 4 November.
