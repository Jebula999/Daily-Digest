## ⚠️ Alerts & outages

**SANParks firefighters, supported by two helicopters, fought fires on Devil's Peak and at Noordhoek Wetlands in Table Mountain National Park on Wednesday 25 November, and closed all trails on Devil's Peak between the end of Tafelberg Road and Rhodes Memorial.** The Devil's Peak fire sent a large plume of smoke over Vredehoek. Ground crews from the park's contractor, NCC Environmental Services, were on the mountain, Volunteer Wildfire Services was called in, and rangers helped visitors leave the Woodstock Caves. A separate ground team was sent to the Noordhoek Wetlands fire. SANParks had not given a cause or the area burnt, and had not said whether the fires were contained when the latest updates were published. Hikers should check that the trails have reopened before heading up, and keep clear of firefighting crews.

**Brackenfell and Brackenfell South are without water from 10:00 to 18:00 today, Thursday 26 November, for planned City maintenance.** The affected areas include Vredekloof, Vredekloof East, Arauna, Morgenster, Morgenster Heights, St Kilda, Marlborough Park, Eden Park, St Micheals and Okavango Park. Zero-pressure tests in Tamboerskloof and Gardens from 10:00 to 17:00 may also leave some streets with low pressure or no water; the City says it cannot predict which streets. No further shutdowns are listed for the weekend.

## 🚨 Safety

**A couple say an object they believe was a rock was thrown into the path of their car on the N1 near the Milnerton and Paarden Eiland turn-off on Tuesday 24 November, bursting a tyre, Cape Town ETC reports.** They saw several men under a nearby bridge, did not stop, and drove to a petrol station in Paarden Eiland. Neither was hurt. Councillor Emre Uygun, who shared their account, said he would ask the City's Safety and Security Directorate to check CCTV footage. No police statement has been reported. Motorists who experience something similar should report it to SAPS; City emergency services can be reached on 021 480 7700.
{: #n1-rock data-place="N1, Paarden Eiland" }

> [Cape Town ETC](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/safety-concerns-raised-after-alleged-rock-throwing-incident-on-cape-towns-n1/)

**NSRI Hout Bay rescued an American tourist at Maori Bay, near Sandy Bay, on Monday 23 November after he fell into the sea while trying to climb onto the wreck of the BOS400, The Citizen reports.** He was caught in rip currents, reached the wreck, then went back into the water for a phone left on the rocks. Rescuers treated him for hypothermia and cuts on the shore and took him by sea to the Hout Bay base; he needed no further treatment. The NSRI warned that the wreck and the water around it are not safe and that no one should try to board it.
{: #bos400-rescue data-place="Sandy Bay, Llandudno" }

## 🏛️ Local government

**A full bench of the Western Cape High Court in Cape Town on Wednesday 25 November set aside the Section 89 panel report that found President Cyril Ramaphosa had a case to answer over the Phala Phala theft, Daily Maverick reports.** Judge President Nolwazi Mabindla-Boqwana, reading a summary, said the panel had misunderstood its role and made errors of law. Proceedings based on the report, including Parliament's impeachment process, were declared invalid. Full reasons are still to come, and the ATM said it was consulting lawyers about an appeal. The Presidency said the President would study the judgment.

## 🩺 Health & community

**A specialised Sexual Offences Court was due to be launched at the Khayelitsha Magistrate's Court on Wednesday 25 November, at the start of the 16 Days of Activism, the Department of Justice said.** The court has private waiting areas for complainants, rooms where children and vulnerable witnesses can testify by closed-circuit TV, and court preparation officers and intermediaries to support them. The department says the aim is to spare survivors further trauma, avoid delays and improve conviction rates. Deputy Justice Minister Andries Nel said no survivor should have to face the justice system alone.

## 🌊 Environment

**The St James spring on Main Road, which kept flowing through the 2018 drought when hundreds of people collected water there each day, has been dry since August 2025, and the City still does not know why, GroundUp reports.** The City told GroundUp this month that inspections have been done and that it is securing a hydrologist, but it has announced no findings. One resident, a chemical engineer, believes re-paving around the outlet may be to blame; others have raised the possibility of a borehole uphill drawing on the source, or a geological shift. A nearby spring at Condover House appears to be still flowing. Level 1 water restrictions start on Tuesday 1 December.
{: #st-james-spring data-place="Main Road, St James" }
