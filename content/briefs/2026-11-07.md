## 🗳️ Elections 2026

**With about 84% of Cape Town's votes counted at 20:00 on Friday 6 November, the DA had 58.84%, ahead of the ANC on 12.04% and the Patriotic Alliance (PA) on 3.82%, The Citizen reports.** The Freedom Front Plus had 3.58% and the EFF 3.5%. The figures are close to the 58% the DA projected on Thursday, and to its 58.26% in 2021. The IEC's Western Cape electoral officer, Michael Hendrickse, said counting was finished in every municipality in the province except the City of Cape Town, SABC News reports; nationally, 91% of results were complete on Friday evening and the IEC hoped to finish early this morning, Saturday 7 November. Results remain provisional until the IEC declares them on Sunday 8 November.

## 🚨 Safety

**The City received 344 reports about fireworks between 3 and 5 November, compared with 156 over the same days last year, 243 of them on Guy Fawkes night itself, Cape Town ETC reports.** Complaints came from areas including Saxonsea, Lentegeur, Hanover Park, Surrey Estate, Manenberg, Mamre, Bonteheuwel and The Hague, along with some stone-throwing. Law Enforcement spokesperson Wayne Dyason said officers struggled to catch those responsible as groups moved around, and asked residents to keep reporting fireworks being let off. The Cape of Good Hope SPCA said its eight inspectors took 81 calls overnight, up from 51, and that a dog in Delft had to be put down after it was impaled on a security gate while trying to escape the noise.
{: #guy-fawkes }

> [Cape Town ETC](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/cape-town-sees-rise-in-guy-fawkes-incidents-and-animal-cruelty/)

**A 69-year-old man was arrested and charged with animal cruelty after two dead dogs were found at a property in Factreton on Thursday 5 November, the SPCA said.** Inspectors went out after a complaint at about 15:00 and found one dog in a water tank and another under a tree. The man, taken to Kensington police station, told inspectors the dogs had been sick and that he had put them in the tank to bathe them. The SPCA has taken the bodies for post-mortems. He was expected in the Cape Town Magistrates' Court on Friday.

## 🏠 Housing & development

**Deputy Mayor Eddie Andrews has ruled out rent control in Cape Town, telling Daily Maverick's Politically Aweh there was "no chance" of it.** Average rent in the city is about R12,500 a month, up nearly 10% in a year, and the city has close to 27,000 Airbnb listings, according to Daily Maverick. The Rent Control Group is asking for limits on how much rents can rise rather than a freeze, a cap on short-term rentals in the CBD, an affordable housing policy, and for national government to bring an already-passed amendment to the Rental Housing Act into force. The group accepts the mayor's argument that rents follow supply and demand, but says the state has tools it is not using.
{: #rent }

> [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-11-06-why-is-rent-so-expensive-in-cape-town-and-would-rent-control-help/)

## 🩺 Health & community

**The provincial Orthotic and Prosthetic Centre in Pinelands now produces 3D-printed spinal braces in about 48 hours, down from about two weeks, the Department of Health and Wellness said on Thursday 5 November.** Patients are scanned standing up, clinicians adjust the scan digitally and the brace is printed, at less than half the cost of a plaster of paris brace. It is used for conditions such as scoliosis, TB of the spine, compression fractures and pigeon chest. The department invested about R5.5 million in the equipment in April, and six patients have been fitted so far. Patients need a referral from a specialist or medical officer and are seen by appointment.
{: #spinal-braces data-place="Pinelands" }

## 🌊 Environment

**A killer whale tagged off Simon's Town on 30 October, the first such tagging in South Africa, has travelled up the west coast to the Northern Cape, the research group Sea Search says.** The female, one of a pair first seen in the Eastern Cape in early October, passed Llandudno and Table Bay before heading north past Saldanha, Lamberts Bay and Kleinsee, Cape Town ETC reports. The tagging, done under government and university permits, is part of a wider study of where killer whales go, what they eat and how they interact with boats and fisheries.
{: #orca data-place="Simon's Town" }

> [Cape Town ETC](https://www.capetownetc.com/news/killer-whale-false-bay/)

**Baboon rangers in the southern Peninsula are stretched thin, Daily Maverick reports from a recent raid in Red Hill, where the Groot Olifantsbos troop broke into a pigsty while the rest of the ranger team was pushing another troop out of Scarborough.** Four rangers cover that troop of more than 50 baboons. A survey of affected suburbs found residents trust the rangers more than the City, CapeNature, SANParks or advocacy groups. The article notes that the Waterfall and Seaforth troops in Simon's Town have been earmarked for a sanctuary, while new fencing is planned to help keep other troops out of town.
{: #baboons data-place="Red Hill, Simon's Town" }

> [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-11-05-cape-towns-baboon-rangers-the-unsung-heroes-of-human-wildlife-conflict/)
