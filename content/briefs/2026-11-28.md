## ⚠️ Alerts & outages

**The City is asking all residents to use at least 10% less water from Tuesday 1 December, when Level 1 water restrictions take effect, Cape Town ETC reports.** Angus McKenzie, the new mayoral committee member for water and sanitation, described it as an early step to avoid harsher restrictions later, and said the city's water use has only in recent years returned to the levels seen before the 2015–2018 drought. The City says it is also stepping up leak detection and repairs, pressure management, pipe and meter replacement and the use of treated effluent, and will adjust its response as it monitors rainfall, dam levels and demand.

## 🏛️ Local government

**Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis announced his new mayoral committee at the Civic Centre on Friday 27 November, promising five things: safer communities, more jobs, reliable and affordable services, more affordable homes and cleaner public spaces.** Deputy Mayor Xanthea Limberg keeps energy. Angus McKenzie takes water and sanitation from Zahid Badroodien, who is going to Parliament, and Kabelo Kgobisa (corporate services) and Mikhail Manuel (spatial planning and environment) are new to the committee, Daily Maverick reports. The other members are Theresa Uys (finance), JP Smith (safety and security), Carl Pophaim (human settlements), James Vos (economic growth), Rob Quintas (urban mobility), Grant Twigg (urban waste) and Francine Higham (community services and health). Eddie Andrews is the new chief whip, and Felicity Purchase was elected Speaker at the first council sitting. Pophaim said he would take social housing funding backlogs up with the national human settlements department and National Treasury. The DA holds its Western Cape provincial elective congress today, Saturday 28 November.

**An appeal against the approval of a second data centre at 90 Pallotti Road, King David Country Club, part of the King Air Industria development, goes to the City's Planning Appeals Advisory Panel on Wednesday 2 December.** The appellants, among them the Legal Resources Centre, question the process and raise the facility's water and electricity use; the developer says its existing water allocation is enough because the centre will be air-cooled. The panel, which advises the mayor on planning appeals, will also hear 14 other appeals, most against penalties for unauthorised building work, including R400,000 for a 16-room boarding house in Algoa Road, Milnerton. ## 🩺 Health & community

**The Constitutional Court on Friday 27 November dismissed the Western Cape Premier's challenge to the National Health Insurance Act by six votes to five, and unanimously dismissed a separate challenge by the Board of Healthcare Funders, GroundUp reports.** The province argued that public participation in the National Council of Provinces had been inadequate. The majority found that people had a reasonable opportunity to influence the law; the minority found that the NCOP committee had treated provincial mandates mechanically, and that the Western Cape's 635-page report was dealt with in a separate 35-minute meeting. The NHI Act, signed in May 2024, is not yet in operation, and further legal challenges are expected. No costs order was made.
{: #nhi-concourt }

> [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/nhi-survives-first-two-constitutional-challenges/)

**About 50 older people, most of them women, marched in Khayelitsha on Friday 27 November to draw attention to elder abuse, GroundUp reports.** The march was organised by Ikamva Labantu, a non-profit that works with older people in Cape Town's townships, as part of the 16 Days of Activism. A social worker with the organisation said some older people are abused financially, emotionally and physically, including relatives taking their SASSA grants, and that red tape makes it hard to place them in safe care. Two residents of Site B described being threatened by relatives over their grant money. Ikamva Labantu urged people to report abuse.

## 🚨 Safety

**An 80-year-old retired pastor is in hospital after a brick was allegedly thrown at his car as he left a service at the Reformed Church in Crossroads on Sunday 22 November, Cape Town ETC reports, citing The Citizen.** He reportedly lost control of the car and crashed into a house. A church official said he was conscious and able to talk, and further tests were planned. No police statement was included in the report. Motorists have reported similar incidents on the R300 and N2 in recent months; no link between them has been established.

## 🌊 Environment

**Attorneys for the Cape of Good Hope SPCA wrote to the City, SANParks and CapeNature on 16 November saying the SPCA would go to court to stop any further permanent confinement of healthy baboon troops, according to an opinion piece by two Stellenbosch University philosophers in Daily Maverick.** The Waterfall troop from Simon's Town was captured on 13 and 14 November and moved to a sanctuary, and the Seaforth troop is next, though no date has been announced. The authors call for the reports behind the decision to be published, the Seaforth plans paused, and an independent ethics panel set up by January 2027. The mayor's chief of staff argued in Daily Maverick on 23 November that the wildlife centre was the only viable option.
