## 🚨 Safety

**The City closed the Delft Public Transport Interchange on Wednesday morning, 28 October, after violence broke out between the CATA and CODETA minibus taxi associations, and has not said when it will reopen.** Mayoral committee member for urban mobility Rob Quintas said the dispute is over routes between Delft, Epping and Killarney, and that violence had spread to Mfuleni and Gugulethu. City officers have responded with police to shootings at several ranks in Delft, Khayelitsha, Mfuleni and Gugulethu since 27 October. Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis offered SAPS the City's camera footage, surveillance technology and officers, and said officers would stay visible near transport interchanges. The City has not given a death toll. Police opened two attempted murder cases and a case of discharging a firearm after shooting at the Palm taxi rank in Delft on Wednesday, Cape Town ETC reported, quoting a SAPS spokesperson. Provincial education minister Jaco Londt said matric candidates who could not safely reach their exam centre on Wednesday were told to go to the nearest one, and could be admitted up to an hour late. Golden Arrow said its buses were running and it would divert routes if needed. Tip-offs can be given anonymously on 0800 110077.
{: #taxi-violence data-place="Delft" }

**Police are offering a R100,000 reward for information after five people were shot dead and three wounded at a home in Blode Street, Lavender Hill, on Saturday evening, 24 October.** Police said the victims, aged 21 to 33, were having a braai when gunmen opened fire, and they believe the attack was gang-related. No arrests had been reported by Monday. Officers who arrived at the scene were pelted with stones, police said. Information can be given anonymously to Crime Stop on 08600 10111 or through the MySAPS app.

Police have not linked this case to the Lavender Hill shooting.

## 🏠 Housing & development

**The groups behind the Tafelberg case say the City's and Province's plans for inner-city affordable housing fall short of the Constitutional Court's order, GroundUp reported.** In July the court found the sale of the Tafelberg School site in Sea Point unlawful and said both governments must provide well-located affordable housing; the High Court is now supervising that. In reports filed on 2 October, the City said it plans about 28,400 affordable homes on land it has sold or will sell to developers, and about 6,700 have been built across the metro, none of them in the inner city. Ndifuna Ukwazi, for the applicants, argues that private "affordable" housing with an income ceiling of R34,800 a month is no substitute for social housing, and wants the national human settlements minister and the Social Housing Regulatory Authority brought into the case. The City blames national government for not funding social housing on City land since 2021. The 376-unit Salt River Market project is funded but its grant agreement is unsigned, and building was due to start in January 2027.
{: #tafelberg-housing }

> [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/activists-concerned-that-cape-towns-affordable-housing-will-not-be-affordable/)

## 🩺 Health & community

**About 20 housekeepers at Nox Cape Town, which manages about 100 holiday rental properties on the Atlantic Seaboard, began a protected strike outside its Camps Bay office on Wednesday 28 October.** Their union, the Tirisano Transport and Services Workers Union, wants a 10% increase on wages of about R7,000 a month; the company has offered 5%. Managing director Nick Taylor said the company pays at or above the market rate and that more than 75% of its housekeepers have accepted the offer.
