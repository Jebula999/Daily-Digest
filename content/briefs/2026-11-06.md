## 🗳️ Elections 2026

**The DA declared victory in Cape Town at noon on Thursday 5 November, projecting about 58% of the vote, but the IEC says only its verified results are final and plans to announce them on Sunday 8 November.** When Geordin Hill-Lewis made the claim, only 24% of the metro's voting stations had been counted, The Citizen reports; in 2021 the DA won 58.26% in the city. With 11% of Cape Town votes counted, the IEC dashboard showed the DA on 59.7%, the ANC on 12.2% and the Patriotic Alliance (PA) on 4.4%, according to City Vision. By Thursday evening the PA was the third-largest party in Cape Town and the second-largest in the province, SABC News reported, up from sixth place in 2021. Across the Western Cape the DA had 53.2% at 18:00 on Thursday, slightly below its 2021 result, and was leading without a majority in 14 councils, which will need coalitions, Daily Maverick reports. IEC chair Mosotho Moepya said most counting was finished and that the remaining work is capturing and checking results. Parties have until today, Friday 6 November, to lodge objections.

**Police arrested four people aged 16 to 20 after officers were attacked with stones and beer bottles near the Hanover Park civic centre voting station on Wednesday 4 November, police said.** Anti-Gang Unit members had stepped in when two groups were throwing stones at each other, and one group then turned on them, People's Post reports. One officer was hurt on the hand and police fired three warning shots. Police said voting was not disrupted. A case of public violence has been registered, and the suspects are expected in the Athlone Magistrates' Court once charged.

## 🏛️ Local government

**The South African Human Rights Commission has asked the Western Cape High Court to stop the City transferring 49 properties it auctioned in February, saying it ignored the need for affordable and social housing, GroundUp reports.** The commission wants the sales halted until the court reviews the decisions, and then set aside and sent back to the City. The 28 residential sites include land in Mitchells Plain, Maitland, Goodwood, Kraaifontein, Somerset West and Durbanville. Three high-value sales, including the Good Hope Centre, still need a final council vote. Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis said the City will oppose the case, called it electioneering, and said land sales this term had raised R628 million for services. The City has until early December to file its papers; no hearing date has been set.

## 🏠 Housing & development

**Mayor Geordin Hill-Lewis, as appeal authority, has still to rule on an appeal against the planning tribunal's approval of Equinix data centres on land opposite Cape Town International Airport, Daily Maverick reports.** The appellants, the Legal Resources Centre and the group Foxglove, say studies on water, energy and the environment have been put off to later stages, and that commitments on cooling and renewable energy are not binding. The developers' consultants argue that the application deals only with subdividing and rezoning land, and that existing 2018 environmental and water authorisations cover the project. The appeal hearings were held in private. The Legal Resources Centre may seek a High Court review, depending on the ruling.

## 🚨 Safety

**Metro Police arrested two people aged 20 to 25 in the city centre on Thursday 5 November in what police describe as an online dating scam syndicate, The Citizen reports.** Officers acting on a tip-off about a vehicle stopped it in the CBD, and a search of a residence with SAPS followed. They seized cash, bank and ID cards, cellphones, four card machines, jewellery, drugs and false number plates. Charges include conspiracy to commit fraud and offences under the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act. Police asked anyone with information to come forward.

## 🩺 Health & community

**Twelve matric candidates in the Western Cape have been caught cheating in the National Senior Certificate exams, six with phones or other devices and six with crib notes, the education department said.** The exams have passed the halfway mark, with 95 of 190 papers written. Education MEC Jaco Londt warned that candidates caught with prohibited items could be barred from writing for up to three exam sessions, and advised learners to arrive at least an hour early given violence and transport disruptions in some areas.
