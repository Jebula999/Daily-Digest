## ⚠️ Alerts & outages

**Several areas will have no water for most of Tuesday 3 November during planned work, and shut-offs start on Monday in Kuils River and Gordon's Bay.** On Monday 2 November, Andy Porter Street and nearby streets in Kuils River are off from 09:00 to 16:00, and streets around Dennehof Street in Gordon's Bay may have low pressure or no water from 09:00 to 19:00. On Tuesday, parts of Steenberg and of Fish Hoek around Kommetjie Main Road are off from 08:00 to 20:00, Wireless Road in Kommetjie from 08:00 to 19:00, and the area between De Wet Road, Exeter Lane and Ottery Road in Ottery from 09:00 to 19:30. Kleinvlei, Spandau, Perm Gardens, Forest Glade, Forest Heights, Park Village, Somerset Heights and Rosedale are off from 09:00 to 19:00. On Thursday 5 November, Brackenfell Heights, Springbok Park and Brackenfell Central are off from 09:00 to 18:00. The City's notice also lists pressure tests that may cut supply and warns that work can run late. It advises storing water in clean, sealed containers, and posts updates and tanker locations on @CityofCTAlerts.

## 🚨 Safety

**The City said on Saturday 31 October that minibus taxis are expected to run for Monday morning's commute, and that the six-month suspension of the CATA Langa association is not a blanket suspension of taxi services in Langa.** The suspension stops the association applying for or renewing operating licences and routes. It does not affect operators with valid licences and route permits. Mayoral committee member for urban mobility Rob Quintas said talks with the industry confirmed commuters could rely on taxis on Monday. Police, City enforcement teams and provincial safety services are to stay visible on routes and at ranks and stations. Provincial and City officials are due to meet CATA and CODETA leaders on Monday 2 November. No reopening date for the Delft interchange, closed on 28 October, had been reported by Sunday morning.
{: #taxi-monday }

**Parliament's select committee on education has asked affected scholar transport operators to report to the Western Cape Education Department, so it can record how many learners the taxi violence affected.** Committee chair Makhi Feni said on Saturday that vehicles used for scholar transport were among those targeted as end-of-year exams began. He asked what the department plans for learners who missed an exam or arrived late, and said counselling and safe travel should be priorities. The Citizen's report gives no figures for affected learners.

## 🗳️ Elections 2026

**Special voting runs on Monday 2 and Tuesday 3 November from 08:00 to 17:00, for voters whose applications were approved, and polling day is Wednesday 4 November, from 07:00 to 21:00.** Nationally, about 2.6 million applications were approved: 678,000 for home visits and 1.9 million for voting at a station. Voters need a smart ID card, a green barcoded ID book or a valid temporary identity certificate. ## 🚌 Getting around

**The City has taken delivery of its first seven battery-electric MyCiTi buses, with 13 more due this month and the last 17 between January and March 2027.** With a prototype that arrived in July, that makes 38 buses. The first 21 are planned for the N2 Express, which links Mitchells Plain and Khayelitsha with the city. No start date has been given. Each 12-metre Volvo bus carries 59 passengers, and the bodies were designed in South Africa and built in Gauteng. Further purchases depend on national funding.

## 🩺 Health & community

**Western Cape health minister Mireille Wenger has asked national transport minister Barbara Creecy for urgent steps to speed up imported medicines at South African ports, particularly Durban.** The delays are adding to existing shortages at the province's health facilities, according to Cape Town ETC. Imported medicines also need three to four weeks of quality testing, which Wenger said should not be cut short. Staff are finding alternatives and moving stock between facilities, but that is getting harder as shortages continue. The report does not name the medicines or say how many patients are affected.

## 🌊 Environment

**The dams supplying Cape Town were 73.3% full on Friday 30 October, below the 80% the City has said it wants at the end of the rainy season, and water users are due to hear this month whether restrictions will follow.** Cape Town ETC reported on 23 October that precautionary restrictions from about 1 December were under discussion between the City and the national water department, and that this was not a final decision. Further assessments were expected in early November. Separately, the City's annual report for July 2025 to June 2026 again rates its tap water as excellent against the national drinking water standard.
