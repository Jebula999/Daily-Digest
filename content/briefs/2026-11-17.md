## ⚠️ Alerts & outages

**Cape Town will move to Level 1 water restrictions from Tuesday 1 December, Cape Town ETC reports, citing the City's comparison table of restriction levels.** Under Level 1, gardens may be watered only on Tuesdays and Saturdays, for up to an hour, and only before 09:00 or after 18:00, whether by sprinkler, hosepipe with a trigger nozzle, drip irrigation, bucket or borehole. Hosing down paving is banned, pools may be topped up but must be covered when not in use, and commercial car washes must recycle at least half their water. Dams stood at 70.4% on 16 November, 13.4 percentage points lower than a year ago, and daily use of 951 million litres is 91 million litres above the City's target; the City is asking everyone to use at least 10% less and suggests a household guide of 85 litres per person a day. City water and sanitation executive director Leonardo Manus said the City "cannot afford to wait" until dams are critically low. The City's water restrictions web page still says no restrictions are in place and does not yet give a start date, so watch for its formal notice. Exemptions can be applied for through the City.
{: #level-1 }

**The national Department of Water and Sanitation is cutting allocations from the Western Cape Water Supply System for the 2026/27 water year by 7% for domestic and industrial users and 15% for irrigation, SAnews reports.** The system's six linked dams supply Cape Town, the West Coast, several towns and farms, and held 72.5% on 2 November against 84.4% a year earlier and a 10-year average of about 84.5%. Water and Sanitation Minister Pemmy Majodina said the cuts would save about 56.8 million cubic metres a year, and warned that restrictions could follow in 2027 if full allocations continued.
{: #allocation-cuts }

> [SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/western-cape-water-users-face-allocation-cuts-amid-el-nino-risks)

**The Faure Water Treatment Plant shuts down from 07:00 tomorrow, Wednesday 18 November, until 18:00 on Thursday 19 November, and the City asks residents and businesses to use less water while supply is redirected from the Steenbras and Blackheath pipelines, Cape Town ETC reports.** Today, parts of Lotus River and Grassy Park are due to be without water from 09:00 to 18:00, and Welgedacht, Selborne Slot, Oude Westhof, Van Riebeeckshof and Kanonberg from 20:00 tonight to 05:00 on Wednesday. Woodstock, Salt River and Observatory may be without water on Thursday from 09:00 to 19:00 for a bulk main repair in Sir Lowry Road.
{: #water-work }

## 🌦️ Weather

**The South African Weather Service has a heat advisory for very hot to extremely hot conditions in the City of Cape Town and on the West Coast today and tomorrow, Tuesday 17 and Wednesday 18 November, The Citizen reports.** SAWS also has a yellow level 1 warning for damaging winds from Table Bay to Cape Agulhas today, which could damage temporary structures and make conditions difficult at sea, and fire danger is extremely high in the Matzikama and Bergrivier areas up the West Coast. Cape Town ETC's forecast gives a city high of about 30°C today with south-south-easterly gusts to 52km/h and an extreme UV index. Drink water, stay out of the midday sun, check on older neighbours, and never leave children or pets in parked cars.

## 🚨 Safety

**Police have launched a manhunt after a 22-year-old man was shot dead in a street in Blue Downs on Sunday 15 November, SABC News reports.** According to police, occupants of a car that pulled up next to him opened fire, and he was declared dead at the scene. Police spokesperson Wesley Twigg said the circumstances are being investigated and no arrests have been made.

## 🏛️ Local government

**Bo-Kaap residents marched on Wednesday 11 November to call on the City to tighten the rules for tour operators in the neighbourhood, Cape Town ETC reports, citing TygerBurger.** The Bo-Kaap Civic and Ratepayers Association says tour buses and growing visitor numbers cause congestion and illegal parking on its narrow streets, and wants regulated tourism, traffic management, local guides and more economic benefit for residents. Its vice-chairperson, Ebrahim Christian, said "our homes are not tourist attractions". The City restricted large tour buses from the area in April 2025. No City response to the march was reported.
{: #bo-kaap data-place="Bo-Kaap" }
