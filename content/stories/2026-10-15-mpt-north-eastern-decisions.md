The **Municipal Planning Tribunal (MPT)** is the City panel of officials and outside experts that decides rezonings, subdivisions and departures from building rules, and sets penalties for building without approval. Its north-eastern branch covers areas such as Bellville, Parow, Goodwood, Kraaifontein, Kuils River and Delft. It met online on Tuesday 13 October. Every item below was approved, almost all unanimously.

## New flats and businesses

- **Belgravia, Bellville (Ward 10):** two neighbouring properties, at **22 and 27 Petrusa Street**, were rezoned from single residential to General Residential 2 to allow blocks of flats. Their side building lines were relaxed from 4.5m to between 1.5m and 2.47m.
- **Panorama, Parow (Ward 1):** **86 Panorama Road** was rezoned from residential to local business for professional offices, with a second driveway allowed.
- **Monte Vista, Goodwood (Ward 1):** title-deed restrictions were removed at **106 Monte Vista Boulevard**, and the property was rezoned from residential to General Business 1.
- **Cravenby, Goodwood (Ward 25):** title-deed and township restrictions were lifted at **12 Cravenby Street**, alongside a consent use and a permanent departure.
- **Vredelust, Bellville (Ward 2):** a **five-bedroom guest house** was approved at 5 Keerweder Street.
- **Edgemead (Ward 5):** a plot at 2 Koornhoop Road may be subdivided into portions of 304m² and 418m². Any new building on the smaller portion first needs a site development plan that addresses keeping mature trees.

## Penalties for building without approval

When owners build or use property without permission and then apply to legalise it, the tribunal sets an "administrative penalty" before the application can go ahead. On Tuesday it set nine:

## Why it matters

Rezonings like the ones in Belgravia are how older single-house suburbs gradually gain more homes. Neighbours who object to applications usually get a chance to comment during the advertising stage, before an application reaches the tribunal. Decisions can be appealed, and the City's Planning Appeals Advisory Panel advises on those appeals.
