The City's General Appeals Committee, which hears appeals against decisions taken by subcouncils and officials, met online on **Friday 30 October**. Both items were appeals against subcouncils that had refused to renew extended liquor trading hours. Extended hours are applied for each year, separately from the liquor licence itself. In both cases the committee upheld the appeal and approved the hours.

## What was decided

| Premises | Hours applied for | Subcouncil decision | Appeal result | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Avenue Liquors, Stop n Shop Centre, 198–202 Horak Street, Peerless Park West, Kraaifontein (Erf 11432) | Off-consumption, 18:00–20:00, Monday to Saturday | Refused by Subcouncil 2, 26 August 2026 | Upheld; hours approved | 4 in favour |
| Club 7, 97A Voortrekker Road, Parow (Erf 7596) | On-site consumption, 02:00–04:00 | Refused by Subcouncil 6, 17 September 2026 | Upheld; hours approved | 6 in favour |

## Kraaifontein

The store's representative told the committee it had traded with extended hours since 2015 without significant complaints, that it employs 11 people and that much of its trade falls between 18:00 and 20:00. The subcouncil had cited traffic, public drinking, noise, litter and safety concerns, and the local civic association had lodged a detailed objection.

Members were split. The chairperson argued for dismissing the appeal, pointing to community opposition and complaints of public drinking and nuisance, and one other member agreed. Four members voted to uphold it, saying the objections did not show that this store's customers caused the problems and that other outlets nearby also have extended hours. The committee's stated reasons were that the hours would not affect the surrounding area and that the outlet meets the zoning and licence requirements.

The chairperson also raised concern that parts of the appeal papers had been so heavily redacted that some pages were unusable, and asked for clarity on the City's redaction practice.

## Parow

The club's representative said Law Enforcement and the designated liquor officer had received no complaints in the past year and supported renewal, and that neighbouring businesses and residents had written in support. The committee had upheld a similar appeal by the same applicant in 2025. Members said the subcouncil's refusal was poorly supported by evidence, though one member said future applications should make a stronger public-interest case. The resolution notes that no complaints or objections were received from Law Enforcement, SAPS or the surrounding community.

## What happens next

Because extended hours must be renewed every year, both premises will need to apply again, and each application is assessed on its own merits. The committee's next scheduled meeting is on **Friday 13 November**.
